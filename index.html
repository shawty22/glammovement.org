Manifesto
AI is becoming part of the infrastructure of everyday life. But we are rapidly accepting a strange premise: that intelligence must live somewhere else. We send our conversations, photographs, documents, memories and questions away to enormous machines, and receive intelligence back as a service. It doesn't have to be this way. The technology now exists to put increasingly capable intelligence where we are: on our computers, our phones, in our homes, our businesses and our communities. This is the case for local intelligence.
1

Privacy by Default

We are entering an age of machines capable of understanding extraordinary amounts about us: our words, our images, our documents, our relationships and increasingly the details of our lives. There is no reason that every interaction with an intelligent machine should require sending that information somewhere else. Local inference offers another possibility: the intelligence comes to the data, rather than the data automatically going to the intelligence. Privacy should not be an advanced setting. It should be the default architecture wherever it is technically possible.

2

Intelligence Should Be Transparent

We are increasingly asking machines to reason, create and make decisions without knowing exactly what is running underneath them. Local AI gives us an opportunity to change that relationship. When models, weights, software and data pipelines can be examined, tested and operated offline, intelligence becomes something we can investigate rather than simply trust. Local does not automatically mean transparent. But local makes a much greater degree of transparency possible. If we are going to live with intelligent machines, we should be able to look inside them.

3

Not Every Query Needs a Hyperscale Datacenter

We have developed a strange habit of throwing every computational problem at the largest machine available. But summarizing a document does not necessarily require a planetary-scale infrastructure. Neither does searching a private archive, organizing photographs, translating a conversation, analyzing a spreadsheet or helping write some code. Smaller models are becoming remarkably capable. Quantization and other techniques are making them dramatically more efficient, while hardware that already exists on our desks and in our pockets can run increasingly sophisticated AI. Not every thought needs a datacenter.

4

The User Should Have More Control

An intelligent system should not automatically have access to everything around it. Local systems allow us to define much clearer boundaries around what an AI can see, remember, access and communicate with. Can it reach the internet? Can it read your files? Can it act on your behalf? Can you isolate it from the rest of your system? Can you simply pull the plug? These are questions of agency, not just engineering. As machines become more capable, our ability to constrain them becomes more important. Capability without control is a poor bargain.

5

We Deserve Private Intelligence

Individuals deserve it. Families deserve it. Communities deserve it. Imagine an intelligent system that understands your household without exporting your household. A system that knows your family's documents, photographs, recipes, history and routines while those things remain yours. Imagine a community building intelligence around its own knowledge without having to surrender that knowledge to a distant platform. We have spent decades accepting surveillance as the hidden price of convenience. AI gives us an opportunity to stop making that bargain.

6

Local Can Become the New Default

The assumption used to be that computing happened on our machines and the network extended their capabilities. AI has inverted that relationship. Increasingly, the assumption is that intelligence lives in the cloud and our devices are merely windows into it. There is nothing inevitable about that architecture. For many tasks, the first question should simply become: can this run here? Local when possible. Edge when useful. Cloud when necessary. The point is not to reject remote infrastructure. The point is to make local intelligence a genuine choice rather than an afterthought.

7

Intelligence Should Be Democratized

William Gibson famously observed that the future is already here; it is just not evenly distributed. AI makes that problem particularly visible. The most capable systems currently depend on extraordinary concentrations of capital, computing power and infrastructure. Local AI offers another route: intelligence that can operate without permanent connectivity, that can run on relatively modest hardware, and that can be deployed where networks are unreliable, expensive, unavailable or deliberately disrupted. A local system powered by independent energy could operate in a remote community, a school, a disaster zone or a climate-affected region without waiting for access to a hyperscale provider. The point isn't to give everyone the same AI. It is to give people the ability to build the intelligence they actually need.

8

Custom Intelligence Instead of Cookie-Cutter Intelligence

The world is not one market. People have different languages, cultures, environments, professions, histories and problems. Why should everyone use essentially the same intelligence? Local systems can be shaped around a particular person, organization or community. They can incorporate local knowledge, specialize in particular work and be constrained according to the needs of the people using them. Instead of adapting ourselves to a corporate AI product designed for the largest possible audience, we can build systems that adapt to us. Intelligence should be customizable because the world is not standardized.

9

We Don't Need a God in the Cloud for Every Human Task

There is a tendency to frame the future of AI as a race toward ever larger and more general intelligence. But most of human life isn't asking for a digital god. We need something to find a document, understand a contract, sort photographs, translate a conversation, analyze a spreadsheet, write some code, search an archive, make music or help us think. These are meaningful forms of intelligence, but they don't necessarily require an omniscient machine on the other side of the planet. The goal isn't maximum intelligence everywhere. The goal is appropriate intelligence, available where it is needed.

10

The Future May Be a Network of Small Intelligences

What happens when intelligence becomes cheap enough, efficient enough and portable enough that it doesn't need to live in one place? A laptop can be intelligent. A phone can be intelligent. A home can be intelligent. A vehicle can be intelligent. A community can be intelligent. These systems can operate independently. They can remain offline. They can share selectively. They can collaborate when useful and disconnect when necessary. Millions of local intelligences could form something very different from today's model: not one machine thinking for everyone, but many machines thinking with us. That future is no longer science fiction. The hardware exists. The models exist. The software exists. What we need now is the culture to use it.

We believe intelligence should be closer to the people who use it: private where it needs to be, transparent where it can be, local where it makes sense, custom where it matters and distributed by design. This is not about putting AI in a smaller box. It is about putting intelligence back within reach.Join the mobilization. · GLAM — Global Local AI Mobilization · v2 · 2026
The environmental cost of cloud AI

Every query.
Every day.
Someone pays.

ChatGPT processes 2.5 billion queries every single day. US data centers consumed 183 terawatt-hours of electricity in 2024 — equivalent to the entire nation of Pakistan. They used 17 billion gallons of water for cooling. Natural gas and coal supply nearly 60% of that power. That number doubles by 2030.

Most people using AI every day have no idea this is happening. The cloud is invisible. The cost is paid by the environment, by communities near data centers, and by a planet that cannot afford it. Local AI eliminates that footprint entirely — and the research proves it works at scale.
