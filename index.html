How global local AI works

Run it close. Share what works.

AI can run on your device or on a group of computers your community shares. That group is a local cluster. Communities can share tools and lessons worldwide while deciding for themselves where their work runs.

Start close to home.

Use smaller models for everyday work, like editing a letter or organizing notes. Pool the hardware so access doesn’t depend on buying a powerful machine.

Reuse good work.

When neighbors ask the same public question, keep a checked answer and its source nearby. Refresh it when the facts change. Keep personal requests separate.

Bring in the cloud when it helps.

Use a larger outside model when the job needs it and the data can go there. Keep basic services available when the connection goes down.

Ten actions for the movement

The GLAMifesto

Let’s build useful AI that our communities can afford, inspect and run. Here’s what we’ll organize around.

Planet

01

Protect our water, land and electric bills.

Try running routine AI on shared machines in buildings we already have. Before a big data center gets a permit or a tax break, make its owners show how much water, land and electricity it needs and who pays for the upgrades. Set limits communities can enforce.

02

Let the neighbors sleep.

Choose local equipment quiet enough to live beside. Measure fans, cooling equipment and backup generators where people actually live, including at night. Set enforceable limits and give neighbors a named operator to call.

03

Build jobs that stay.

Pay people to operate, repair and teach others to use local AI. Before handing out public subsidies, count temporary construction and lasting local jobs separately. Put wages and hiring commitments in writing.

04

Use the AI we already have.

Try smaller local models for everyday work like cleaning up an email or drafting a checklist. Move up to a bigger model when it makes a difference. Count useful work finished and resources used.

Privacy

05

Keep private life private.

Keep sensitive work on devices or local systems people trust. Keep private requests out of shared caches. Show people what would leave their system, ask first, and let them see and delete what’s kept.

06

Give us a say and a switch.

Give people a human way to get help and appeal an AI-assisted decision. Give operators an independent way to pause the AI or cut its cloud connection. Our water, electricity and other essential services should still be operable when that connection is cut.

07

Our knowledge, our rules.

Tribal nations decide how their data and knowledge can be used. Other communities and rights holders should set the terms for material they steward. Build local systems that honor permission, limits on reuse and decisions to withdraw access.

People

08

Pool the machines. Share the keys.

Start shared clusters with neighborhood groups, cooperatives and community institutions. Use the hardware that makes sense, including Apple, NVIDIA and other options. Give people a vote on access, spending and who operates the service.

09

Do the work once. Share what we learn.

If fifty neighbors ask when the town picks up bulky waste, keep the official source and a checked answer nearby. Do the same for public research we have permission to share. Update or retire saved answers when the facts change.

10

Open the code. Show the receipts.

Build with open-source software people can inspect, repair and adapt. Publish the models and licenses, when the system calls the cloud, what it costs, and how it performs. Teach others to run it so the next community can start further ahead.

Research behind the movement

Put the claims to the test.

The Global Local AI Foundation (GLAF) is the research side. Its agenda includes testing which everyday tasks can stay local, what repeat work can be avoided, and whether a local cluster can help operators respond to rogue AI agents while essential services stay under human control.

  1. Water, land and electricity. Compare electricity per completed task, peak load, cooling water and the space needed to deliver comparable services. Count the cluster’s idle draw.

Get involved

Pick one service. Build it together.

GLAM is a founder-led community movement. Bring one thing your community wants AI to help with, or one way you can help build it.

Organize locally.

Bring a neighborhood group, cooperative, school or community institution. Help set the rules and choose a first service.

Build and test.

Help run a node, lend hardware, teach neighbors or collaborate with GLAF on a research question.

Bring your issue.

Bring experience with water, land, noise, jobs, civil rights, privacy or data sovereignty. Help decide what a community should demand and measure.
